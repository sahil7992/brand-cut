Screen rect (comp area, inside the AE chrome, full-res): `crop=780:516:176:664` (the earlier prior-study crop 840:560:130:630 includes AE panel chrome at the top and left, so the new one is tighter; keystone is slight, the top edge of the picture is ~10 px higher on the right). - Song: "DABOOMJIGGLE" by Out of Flux and Eyal Talmudi. librosa tempo 123.0 BPM (period 0.4876 s, best phase 0.245 s) with a swung funk groove (alternative reading 184.6 BPM triplet feel). - Capture caveat: where the comp is quiet, every second video frame is identical (change pattern `#.#.#.`), so the comp plays at 30 fps and the phone records it at 60. Assume the source is a 30 fps composition.

## Summary

A Wise brand film in a light/dark, lime-and-forest-green system. A giant extruded WISE wordmark is trucked across in 0.65 s and cut to the small logo. A browser frame with the real wise.com hero pushes in on a man's photo carrying a live "Sent 133 GBP" chip, which match-cuts to black where "Sent" chips (flag, label, rolling amount) fly in one by one, cluster under the headline "SEND AND SPEND MONEY WORLDWIDE", then flip to light and turn lime as marquee rows under "REAL EXCHANGE RATE". "NO HIDDEN FEES" closes its word gaps; a check badge lifts out as a dot, grows into a globe carrying "A GLOBAL SYSTEM" / "MADE FOR WORLD" in magenta. Hard cut to dark: a lime dot squashes, stretches into a pill, types "SENDING", then a light inversion stacks "PROCESSING." and "DELIVERED" while the camera scrolls up. A lime debit card (with its black wallet) tilts, shrinks, straightens and pushes in under "THE MOST INTERNATIONAL DEBIT CARD". End card: logo on dark then the same logo on light. Arc: brand hit -> product proof (real site, real UI chips) -> promise (no hidden fees) -> process (send, process, deliver) -> product object (card) -> logo.

## Flash profile

Measured on the crop, 160 px wide, 59.94 fps.

| time | change | mean lum before -> after | changed-pixel fraction | note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.65 | up | 0.30 -> 0.48 | 0.37 | giant wordmark -> logo (dark glyphs removed) |
| 1.65 | down | 0.44 -> 0.33 | 0.38 | light logo -> page (bg partly dark) |
| 3.40 | down | 0.30 -> 0.03 | 0.53 | page -> black, 2-3 frames |
| 4.52 | up | 0.09 -> 0.50 | 0.77 | black -> light, 2 frames |
| 11.06 | down | 0.42 -> 0.04 | 0.90 | globe -> dark pill, 1 frame |
| 12.60 | up | 0.09 -> 0.40 | 0.84 | dark -> light inversion, 1 frame |
| 18.57 | down | 0.44 -> 0.02 | 0.88 | card -> dark logo, 1 frame |
| 19.57 | up | 0.02 -> 0.54 | 0.94 | dark -> light logo, 1 frame |

- Full-frame floods (jump >= 0.1 and changed fraction >= 0.25): three dark-to-light cycles (3.40-4.52, 11.06-12.60, 18.57-19.57) plus the two small 0.65/1.65 steps. All are hard 1-3 frame swings of 0.3-0.47 relative luminance, far harsher per cut than reel DesrDGAoX_Q's ramps.
- Max opposing transitions per second: 1 pair per 1.0 s (18.57 down, 19.57 up). Dark holds are 1.1, 1.5 and 1.0 s; the gap from one cycle to the next is >= 6 s. So the piece stays under 3 flashes/s and is about one flood per 7 s, but each is a single-frame strobe-like step; for a brand use at most one inversion cut per 8 s and soften it to a 4-6 frame ramp.
- Background luminance in the comp is bimodal: light 0.40-0.54, dark 0.02-0.09, with almost no mid-tone, which makes each cut read as a flash; the phone's exposure compresses the real values slightly.

## Sound design

- Music only (no SFX layer in the file). RMS runs -11 to -24 dB; the quietest moment is -34 dB at 1.5-1.75 s (the logo hold, just before the 1.65 cut), so there is one dip of silence in 20 s.
- Cuts land on audio accents: 12 of 13 cuts are within +-0.08 s of an onset detected by librosa (3.37, 4.52, 5.55, 7.33, 8.51, 11.09, 12.60, 13.57, 15.05, 17.66, 18.53, 19.52). The rigid 123 BPM grid fits only half of them (errors of 0.10-0.23 s), so the editor cut to the drum accents of a swung groove, not to bar lines.
- Low-band (kick) hits: 0.1, 1.1, 1.8, 2.7, 3.25, 3.75, 5.4, 6.0, 6.7..., a strong hit at 11.0-11.3 (low 37-52) lines up with the globe -> dark pill cut at 11.06, and a heavy hit at 12.6 with the inversion cut.
- Hi-band spikes at 4.7, 6.0, 6.6, 8.3 are percussion shakers/claps.

## Why this does not look AI-generated

1. Real extruded 3D type with a visible side wall that changes with the camera yaw, motion blur on the first moves, and a soft shadow: physically plausible, not a flat gradient.
2. Real product material: real wise.com page and a real photograph; chip UI uses real flags, real currency codes, locale-correct number formats ("1,141 GBP", "$2,000", "2,000CAD").
3. Numbers roll with jitter and then lock to round values: designed micro-detail instead of a smooth count-up.
4. Every element has one clear easing signature: wordmark truck near-linear, chips expo-out, dot->globe expo-in, pill sigmoid stretch with anticipation hold, stack scroll power3-out with a 2 px settle.
5. Squash and stretch on the dot/pill (taller before it stretches), with an extruded side wall on the pill (3D depth).
6. Strict two-color discipline: lime #84E40C-class and forest #0A1A0A on off-white, plus a single magenta accent only on the globe text; no gradients on type.
7. Hard cuts hidden by match: the UK chip survives the page -> black cut; green-on-white colours match across the wordmark -> logo cut.
8. Type is one heavy grotesk, tight tracking, sized to the frame (words fill width) and always aligned to a grid axis (left/right offsets in the stack are intentional).
9. The photographic wrapper (monitor, AE chrome, keyboard, lamp) reads as a real artist's process.

## What would make it look AI

1. A smooth count-up (0 to 400) without jitter, or amounts like "$1,234.56" that do not belong.
2. Flat flag emoji or generic circle avatars instead of real flag artwork, and made-up currency amounts.
3. Chips in a perfect grid with the same entrance for all (same ease, same delay), no overlap or ghosting.
4. A morph from dot to globe done as a crossfade, with no scale ease and no rotation; a globe with texture-less AI continents.
5. Alternating light/dark flashes more often than every few seconds, or on every beat.
6. Soft glassmorphism everywhere instead of matte extruded objects; rainbow gradient text.
7. A card drop with no tilt change, no pull-back, and no wallet to give scale.
