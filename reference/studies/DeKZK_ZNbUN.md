## Summary

A hand-drawn, no-AI "change" film in a restrained paper/black/orange/red system. It opens with a flash of handwriting and a bold "how" typed word by word on a dashed baseline, collapses it to a tiny line, writes the sentence "how do you communicate that you're going through a change?" with pen scribbles and a signature, then bursts through a colour flash into a cyan starburst with floating icons. A head silhouette (gradient-mapped footage, orange to red on black) answers "you dont. / you just show it." A ring of 11 glossy pixel-art objects (vinyl, skateboard, camera, black cat, coin, controller, cap, heart, money, plant, clapperboard) is hit by black ink brush strokes; three one-word cut scenes ("action." with a spinning camera, "intention." with a book and a red ribbon, "curiosity." with a vinyl and red music notes) alternate with white ring scenes; the ring explodes into ink silhouettes; a gradient-mapped hand reaches under "through ones own ability to"; then a heart, camera, book and vinyl each take the place of the missing letters of "L O V E" before the letters scatter on paper and a red pen circles them. Arc: question -> answer in three words -> object world -> reach -> "LOVE".

## Why this does not look AI-generated

1. Real hand-drawn pen strokes with pressure (thick start, thin tail), real signature and loops that are drawn on frame by frame, plus a blurred ghost of the signature as depth of field: inconsistent in the way a hand is.
2. Pixel-art sprites keep a crisp nearest-neighbour grid at any scale (no smoothing), and each has hand-chosen shading and a contact shadow: a designed icon family, not generated images.
3. Photographic base layers: the head and the hand are real footage with gradient maps, so hair strands, edge noise and skin shape are real; when the map drops the original window-light footage shows.
4. Type is lowercase bold grotesk with deliberate typos ("you dont", "ones") and a block caret: reads as a human voice, not a polished AI script.
5. Every reveal is physically motivated: ink hits cause orange bursts at the hit point with a small drop and splatter; icons turn into ink silhouettes in sequence.
6. Contrast of scale: type 23 percent of frame height collapses to 3 percent in 0.4 s; icons 9-22 percent; letters 3 percent: a clear scale hierarchy.
7. Grain, vignette and paper texture on every light scene; dark scenes have a faint noise; no clean digital gradients.
8. Rhythmic editing against a sparse track with lengths varying (0.04 s to 3.2 s): motion lands on accents, silence is used (2.5-3.75 s).
9. Colour restraint: paper, black, orange-red, one cyan star only once.

## What would make it look AI

1. Smooth, uniform pen strokes with identical width and perfect circles, or SVG hand-drawn fonts.
2. Smoothed, anti-aliased "pixel" icons (blurry pixel art) or icons rendered by a diffusion model with inconsistent pixel size.
3. A cyan/purple/pink gradient glow on everything instead of one use of a cool colour.
4. Strobing white/black cuts at 3 per second with no content logic: the opposite of this piece's held bright and dark states (0.3-3 s).
5. Generic stock "growth/change" footage instead of one original silhouette and one hand.
6. Typography with perfect, error-free marketing copy and all-caps or italics; a text caret that never blinks.
7. Floating particles and bokeh orbs that are not tied to any action.
