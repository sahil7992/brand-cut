## Why this does not look AI-generated
1. Every UI value is consistent: the same revenue ($30,797.00), counts (19 customers, 44 calls, 212 leads) appear in the dashboard, cards and chip chain; invented UI would drift.
2. Counters tick with a physical ease-out (geometric decay), with tabular numerals that do not jitter.
3. Per-element timing: caption words appear every 0.2-0.3 s while UI moves free of the beat grid, so motion has two clocks (type and UI).
4. Cursor and click states: hover tint on the nav item, a 2-frame press, an intentional dwell before each click.
5. Shape continuity: chip -> card -> circle -> pill -> caret is one object morphing through the film; the same green dot becomes the logo and the rings. AI-generated video tends to hard-switch objects.
6. Layout discipline: left text column, right UI, same baseline; typography is one family with one highlight device (green chip).
7. Material restraint: gradients only on cards (green to black), wash gradients only at moments of emphasis, a physical notification look, glow only on the focus row.
8. Hard cuts only where a theme changes (black to white at 10.7, white to black at 46.57), both on the beat; everything else morphs.

## What would make it look AI
1. Invented dashboard data that changes between shots or has gibberish labels.
2. Uniform spring easing and bounce on every element and mouse movement in straight lines.
3. Generic glassmorphism cards with random colour blobs, and full-frame flashes on every cut.
4. Stock footage of generic office workers instead of a consistent creator persona (here: one creator at a laptop with one teal and one orange set).
5. Captions that change wording every shot with different fonts and weights.
6. No dwell: UI that moves non-stop with no 0.3-0.5 s reading hold.
