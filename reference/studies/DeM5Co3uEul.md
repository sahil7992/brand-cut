## Summary

A 30 s partner piece for Higgsfield that sells a workflow: take a UI designed in Figma, ask an AI agent (Claude-style chat client with a Higgsfield connector, then a Higgsfield composer) to import it into After Effects as editable layers and animate the transitions, then keep working in After Effects. It opens with an orange pixel mosaic and the line "This is something" in a lime highlighter, then a three-panel feature triptych ("Design in Figma / Import into After Effects / Animate in After Effects"), then a cinematic black-and-white runner clip where the "80%" Pintopay card is selected in Figma and "turn it into a Motion Piece" dissolves it into a counting animation. A long middle act shows real chat UI recreated in motion: "Sooooo......", a "Hello, Saqib" composer with a plus menu and a Higgsfield toggle, a "/use-after-effects" slash command, a hard cut to a white "AI MOTION DESIGNER" title card with a lime cursor, then a Higgsfield composer where the card thumbnail docks and a prompt is typed and sent. The payoff is the After Effects project panel filling in, a zoom out to the AE workspace with a scrubbing counter, three typed lines ("Open the layers / Tweak the timing / Keep working from there") and the lime Higgsfield logo. Palette discipline: near-black with one lime (#d6fc17), one white world, one blue card.

## Why this does not look AI-generated

1. The UI is a real, readable, specific interface: Figma toolbar with correct icons, layer names ("FooterTelegramBadge"), a real AE project with named assets (Wavu_Logo.ai), and a plausible chat client; nothing is invented glyph noise.
2. Motion decisions are per-element: a linear counter at 3 points per frame vs an ease-out flight (36 points in 17 frames) vs a whip pan with horizontal blur. Different curves for different jobs.
3. Lime is the only accent and it is used physically: highlighter boxes with black text, handles, rim light, shimmer bars. No random gradients.
4. Hard edits on beat: 1.00, 14.14, 16.35, 17.23, 21.64, 24.73 land exactly on the 136 BPM grid; the white cut sits on the 14.14 beat.
5. Recreated UI contains behavioural details: caret blink, key-by-key typing speed, hover highlight, toggle flip, press-scale on send (0.92), cursor drop shadow.
6. Depth-of-field used as hierarchy: the connector flyout is defocused until the toggle is the subject, background composer blurs while the menu opens.
7. Real screen recordings (After Effects) mixed with graphic overlays (lime shimmer) so scale and text are crisp; the only generated asset is the runner plate, which is deliberately graded B&W and kept behind a real-looking UI.
8. Dot-matrix and halftone grain on every dark scene tie shots together.
9. Typing is imperfect: "Sooooo......" dots arrive unevenly; titles type with a glitch, not a clean slide.

## What would make it look AI

1. The runner plate carries the "AI" feel (smooth motion, soft face, uncanny hands). Using generated people as the hero, without a UI anchor, reads as generated.
2. A flat grey or white frame used as a glitch on every beat.
3. One-curve-for-everything easing, or bouncing every UI element.
4. UI text in the card at illegible size or with invented words.
5. Heavy all-caps display type everywhere (here "AI MOTION DESIGNER"); it is a house style of this reel and one of the things to avoid for a brand.
6. Over-saturated neon lime on a dark gradient without any physical light source.
7. A dense triptych without reading time: here each panel holds 1.8 s, which is enough only because the type is large.
